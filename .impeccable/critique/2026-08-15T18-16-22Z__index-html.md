## Overall Impression

This is a credible, visually disciplined registration page with a strong opening and thoughtful interaction semantics. The biggest opportunity is not a wholesale redesign; it is to turn the page from a long information stack into a reassuring two-step conversion journey: choose a role, register attendees, reserve the hotel, and know what happens next.

Cognitive load is **moderate: 3 of 8 checklist failures**. Chunking, minimal choices, and working-memory support fail. The biggest choice clusters are six primary navigation links, 13 FAQ questions, five hotel-image actions, and 11 convention photos.

Emotionally, the hero creates anticipation, pricing introduces an early anxiety valley, the keynote and resort restore excitement, repeated “coming soon” language erodes readiness, and the sparse footer ends without a final conversion peak.

## What’s Working

1. **The hero earns attention.** It answers what, when, and where immediately, uses authentic footage, and presents a decisive registration CTA.

2. **The visual language is disciplined.** Navy, teal, lime, coral, serif display type, compact labels, and restrained geometry create a professional campaign identity.

3. **Core semantics are unusually thoughtful.** The page includes a skip link, strong focus indicators, reduced-motion support, keyboard-operated tabs, native dialogs, meaningful image alt text, and new-tab announcements.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The mobile back-to-top control obstructs content and actions

At 390×844, the fixed control overlapped the Owner Registration CTA, pricing text, keynote imagery, FAQ rows, a resort image, and part of **Send Message**. Its mobile height is also 40px, below the common 44px touch target.

**Why it matters:** It directly blocks reading and conversion on the most constrained viewport.

**Fix:** Reserve safe bottom space, hide the control when it intersects actionable content, and use a compact 44×44 control only after meaningful scroll or upward-scroll intent.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt index.html`

### [P1] “Send Message” is a misleading pseudo-submit

The contact form looks like an in-page submission but converts the fields into a `mailto:` URL. It has no sending, success, failure, retry, fallback, or preserved-draft state.

**Why it matters:** A user without a configured email client can complete the form and receive no useful result.

**Fix:** Connect a real endpoint with inline feedback and preserved input. If that is not possible, rename the action to **Open email draft**, display the destination address, and provide a copy-address fallback.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden index.html`

### [P1] Mobile navigation hides destinations without an overflow cue

The six-link navigation becomes a horizontal rail. At 390px, FAQ and Photos are initially offscreen; scrolling the rail can partially clip Register. The two-row sticky header consumes about 124px, roughly 15% of the viewport.

**Why it matters:** Users cannot reliably recognize all available destinations and lose valuable vertical space throughout a 10,000px-plus page.

**Fix:** Keep Register dominant, group the remaining anchors into three destinations plus a labeled More menu, or add unmistakable edge fades and an active-section state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill index.html`

### [P2] Pricing and booking create a mobile memory bridge

Desktop pricing compares well side by side, but mobile stacks three tall cards. Users must remember `$749 + $75/person` while reading `$1,149 + $199/person`; “October 16” has no year. Convention registration and hotel reservation are separate external workflows with no completion model.

**Why it matters:** The highest-stakes decision requires mental arithmetic and remembering state across sites.

**Fix:** Label the amounts as **business fee + attendee fee**, include the full deadline and savings, use a compact mobile comparison, and introduce an explicit checklist: **1. Register attendees → 2. Reserve hotel**.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape index.html`

### [P2] The strongest persuasive evidence arrives late and is visually suppressed

Pricing and compliance language precede the keynote, destination, and attendee proof. Desktop galleries start desaturated, translucent, and slightly blurred until hover. The convention community imagery appears only after the long FAQ.

**Why it matters:** The page asks users to absorb cost and obligation before it fully earns desire and confidence.

**Fix:** Move a compact keynote/community/destination proof band above pricing, show photography in true color, add outcome-oriented captions, and finish with a final registration CTA, deadline, and reassurance.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout index.html`
